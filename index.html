President's Message

2026-2027 Season


TBFC is Back in Action!

Welcome back, fossil fanatics! The school buses are rolling, summer vacations are winding down, and the worst of Florida’s summer heat should be behind us. (Who am I kidding?) Either way, it’s time to dust off those fossil screens, check the snorkel/SCUBA gear, and get the Tampa Bay Fossil Club back into action!

While many of you were hiding from the heat, your TBFC Officers and Directors were busy preparing for another exciting season. We have a full schedule of club meetings, fascinating speakers, field trips, and plenty of opportunities to get outside and hunt for the fossils Florida has been hiding from us for a millennia!

The Peace River Is Calling! - Even with the rain we’ve been getting, the Peace River is still running under six feet at Zolfo Springs. This is good conditions for adult fossil hunters, although you may want to keep the kiddos out of the river for now. Check out Fred Hendshot’s Peace River Report in this edition for details.

Earlier this year, the Peace River reached historically low water levels, creating some excellent opportunities for fossil hunters. We’re looking forward to seeing all the incredible specimens that have been turning up as a result. If you’ve made a great find, don’t keep it to yourself! Bring your best specimens to the next TBFC meeting and show them off during our Show & Tell. You can also share your discoveries with fellow fossil enthusiasts on the TBFC Facebook page.

Before heading out, make sure your fossil-hunting permits are current and that you’re familiar with the latest river conditions. The Peace River can change quickly, especially after heavy rains; so take advantage of the low water levels while conditions remain safe. The fossils aren’t going to find themselves!

Congratulations to Our 2026 Scholarship Winners



I want to congratulate one of the two winners for 2026, Elise Samuelson. Elise attends the University of South Florida and is a Ph.D. student who joined TBFC Advisor Dr. Ryan Carney’s lab as an undergraduate during the summer of 2021.

Of particular interest to Elise are studies relating to anatomy, phylogeny, and morphology. Elise also has special skills and interests in 3D modeling, 3D printing, and digital art, and hopes to be able to incorporate these into her work. She plans to form her dissertation around crocodilian locomotion, specifically looking into forelimb walk cycles by reconstructing the anatomy in a 3D digital environment. With this, the findings can be used to infer and compare forelimb movement in both extinct and extant species of archosaurs.

At one of TBFC’s upcoming meetings, we will present a second scholarship to University of Florida student, Shay Rice, Ph.D.

Be good to one another and stay safe out there.

Good Hunting,
Mike Searle

New Members! - Over the last few meetings, we’ve been thrilled to welcome an increasing number of new members. It’s genuinely exciting to see fresh faces in the crowd, and we look forward to getting to know each of you, both at our meetings and out in the field. Next Meeting Presentation

September 12, 2026
The Great Debate: The Evolution of the Great White Shark
with Eileen Durkin, Fossil Hunter, TBFC Director
7PM IN-PERSON CLUB MEETING at USF Behavioral Science Room #103

I’m getting to the age where everything that happened seems like it was just yesterday, and everyone younger than me looks like a kid. It certainly seems like Eileen joined TBFC just yesterday and that she’s still the young person I met years ago. But the reality is that she’s been a member of TBFC going on 15 years.
,br> Eileen is a TBFC director, she films most of our meetings and has handled our YouTube presence for years. She also helps me navigate the Tweeter and Insta-thing whenever I run into problems that seem to come with being a Boomer. She manages tampabayfossilclub.com and has on occasion provided articles for the Chronicles, “Diving with Durkin.” A few years ago, Eileen organized a field trip to hunt for Bone Valley fossils at a private land site and was able to negotiate a discount for our members too.

Despite some serious medical challenges that might have kept most people out of the water, Eileen persevered and became an accomplished SCUBA diver, also earning her intro cave-diving certification.

I’ve had the pleasure of hunting fossils with Eileen in Florida and all the way out to the Badlands of Nebraska. She brings youthful excitement and enthusiasm to our fossil hunts and is a pleasure to be around.

Eileen has been a wonderful part of TBFC, and I’m grateful for all she has contributed to the club over the years. I’m looking forward to seeing what adventures, and perhaps a few more fossil finds, she has for her September presentation.

Eileen has been fascinated by shark teeth since she began collecting them as an eight-year-old child. What started as a childhood hobby in 1996 has grown into a lifelong passion for sharks and fossil hunting. While her career is in software engineering, she continues to pursue her passion for fossil hunting, sharks, and sharing her adventures with others.

The Great Debate: The Evolution of the Great White Shark - For many years, scientists debated where the modern Great White Shark came from. Was it a descendant of the giant megatoothed sharks like megalodon, or did it evolve from an entirely different group of sharks?

In September’s presentation, Eileen will explore the history of that debate by following the fossil record in search of the ancestors of today's Great White Shark. Along the way, we'll look at the fossil evidence, how scientific thinking has changed over time, and why most researchers now support a different evolutionary path than many people grew up hearing about.

Whether you're new to fossil sharks or have been collecting for years, this presentation will provide a fresh look at one of the most interesting questions in shark paleontology.
